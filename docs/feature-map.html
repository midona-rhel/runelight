OverviewInteractions to review first
  • Indoor lighting is several terms, not one indoor brightness value. Building classification, skylight,

room fill, local lamps, ambient occlusion and area lighting contribute through different paths. Caves take a different lighting branch from buildings in open-sky areas.

  • The lighting master switch is not semantically narrow. The current Sun Lighting switch affects the

branch used for cave lighting, room-fill targeting and night-only lamp weighting.

  • There is more than one definition of shelter. Point lights now use structural placement classes,

which can include fences: this is not yet a guarantee of strictly walls and roofs only. weather shelter uses a separate opaque-geometry representation; room IDs and fog density use other data. A shared word such as roof, inside or hidden does not mean a shared calculation.

  • The day clock and weather clock are independent. Freezing the sun does not freeze every weather phase.
  • Several labels/comments are stale. The detailed guides distinguish declarations, implementations and

suspected intent. Tooltips are not treated as authoritative documentation.

  • Legacy paths still exist. Presence of a class or shader does not mean it participates in the live frame.

The audits flag remaining old light-map/cube code separately from the active structural query.

OverviewPlay and review without changing everything at once

The client is left running for your own testing. No further restarts, scene changes, time changes or benchmark runs are part of this documentation pass. Useful feedback is the location, the visible problem, the relevant setting and what you expected. You do not need to collect a formal test report.

For an indoor-light review, keep the camera and time fixed yourself if useful, compare a window/doorway, a room with a lamp and a room without one, and note whether the location is a building or a cave. The guides explain which settings should affect each term; unexpected differences remain findings to investigate, not evidence to explain away with the harness.

Lighting & indoorsA concrete indoor walkthrough

For an opaque wall inside a detected overworld room, the renderer approximately adds:

  • Sky/ambient light, reduced by skylight exposure and multiplied by area ambient tint and AO.
  • Direct sun or moon light, multiplied by its surface angle, directional visibility, area sun tint and AO.
  • A warm room-fill term, multiplied by AO.
  • Visible imported point lights, each with its own distance falloff and surface angle, also multiplied by AO.
  • Material emission and additional material-specific effects.

The actual code is in composite_frag.glsl, especially the outdoor/cave branch and lampsOnSurface. Fog, wet surfaces, water and final color processing can alter the resulting appearance further.

A closed room at night with no torch is deliberately still lit. Skylight retains a 40% floor of its incoming ambient term even at zero sky exposure. Separately, at the default Interior brightness setting, every detected room receives a warm fill with target luminance 0.40. The room does not need a lamp, an opening or a window to receive that fill. AO can darken it locally. Moonlight only adds where the directional calculation permits it.

Adding a torch adds another term. It does not subtract from the room's artificial fill, and the room target does not react to the number of torches. This can make an already readable room very bright when several authored lamps overlap.

Walking through a doorway combines different transitions. Skylight has spatial smoothing near openings. Room fill is selected directly by the surface's room ID; it has no corresponding doorway-distance fade in the current composite. Fill levels ease over time when their target changes, which is different from spatial fading at a doorway. The room lookup offsets a surface 40 world units along its face normal to select the appropriate side of a wall.

An actual cave takes another path. It receives a fixed ambient contribution of 0.50 plus a directional key of 0.40, modulated by area tints and AO. That key is not the moon. Room fill is targeted to zero and is not added by the cave branch. Imported point lights and material emission can still contribute. Interior brightness is consequently not a general cave-brightness control.

Turning Sun and moon lighting off also chooses that cave-style lighting path. It does not simply remove the sun and leave the same outdoor ambient model in place. It disables the room-fill target and sets the night-only lamp multiplier to its nighttime value. That coupling is implemented behavior, whether or not it matches the setting's intended meaning.

Lighting & indoorsHow a room is detected and assigned

BuildingDetector begins from tiles marked under a roof. It groups connected tiles by plane and roof identity, then splits along wall and diagonal-wall boundaries. Closed doors can split rooms; open doors do not. Fences, gates and low walls are excluded from this room-wall model. Candidate groups smaller than three tiles or larger than 2,000 are rejected; additional open/eave rejection removes some marginal groups. If roof IDs are insufficiently consistent, detection falls back away from relying on them.

BuildingLights captures input on the client thread and builds the wall/roof/room result on a worker. RoomMap supports four planes, floor heights, diagonal tile halves and room IDs up to 4094. Texture packing is also asynchronous; consumers can briefly use the previously uploaded map. This is intentional lifecycle complexity, not evidence that every room update is instantaneous.

rooms.glsl selects the highest floor below the sampled position with a two-world-unit margin. A room ends 420 units above its floor, approximately 3.28 tiles. The normal offset is 40 units, approximately 0.31 tiles. These fixed distances matter around thin walls, low ceilings, diagonals and stacked floors. Roof hiding for the player's camera is not the same thing as declaring the space outdoors.

Room-fill calculation

RoomLight computes one target for all rooms, using sun altitude a in degrees and the setting b = Interior brightness / 100:

clearOutdoor = 2.64 * smoothstep(-4, 10, a) * max(sin(a), 0)
             + 0.80 * smoothstep(-10, 15, a)
dayTarget    = max(0.40, 0.42 * clearOutdoor)
nightShare   = smooth transition from 0 at +6° to 1 at -6°
target       = mix(dayTarget, 0.40, nightShare) * max(b, 0)

At a 45° sun altitude, default brightness gives approximately 1.12 target luminance. At deep night it gives 0.40. At 200% both targets double. These are linear lighting terms; 200% does not imply double the final displayed pixel value. The warm tint is normalized to unit luminance and derives from linear RGB (1, .552, .231).

Changes use exponential easing with a 0.4-second time constant, not a fixed 0.4-second completion time. Frame delta is capped at 0.5 seconds. A fresh scene initializes to target. Same-scene room topology changes try to inherit the dominant overlapping old room's level; rooms without a predecessor can start at zero and ease up.

The target uses a clear-sky approximation, not current cloud cover, current skylight exposure, lamp count or measured room brightness. Its tint is also not multiplied by the area's ambient tint in the composite. This can make room fill respond differently from nearby outdoor light during weather or area transitions.

Lighting & indoorsPoint lights: source, animation and visibility

LampFrame.collectLights collects only SceneLights, whose definitions come from the imported 117 HD light data. There is no second automatic room-lamp generator in this active path. “117 HD only” does not mean stationary lamps only: definitions can attach to world coordinates, object IDs, NPCs, projectiles and graphics objects. This audit does not establish that the checked-in import matches the latest upstream revision.

LightDefinition supports location/area exclusions, parent animation filters, height and rotated offsets, alignment, color, radius, strength, flicker/pulse, schedules, spawn/despawn timing and parent lifetime rules. An object without a matching imported definition does not become a lamp merely because it visually resembles a torch.

OutdoorLight uses horizon and midday blends and a blue moon contribution with at most 0.12 strength. This is separate from the main directional moon term.

Lights are gathered, distance-culled and assigned to screen/depth clusters. LightClusters considers at most the first 1024 input lights, then processes those by importance and caps a cluster at 127 lights. Later, lower-priority candidates lose a slot when that cluster is full. Authored strength times radius influences importance. Large radii and overlapping lights can therefore affect both work and which lights survive a cap; changing these parameters is not purely aesthetic.

Current wall/roof occlusion

lamp.glsl performs a binary segment query from the shaded surface toward a lamp origin. If a captured blocker intersects it, that lamp contributes zero. Otherwise the contribution falls off as (1 - distance / radius)^2 inside the radius and is zero outside. Opaque diffuse response also uses the smooth surface normal. There is no soft point-shadow filter or per-light cubemap in this active path.

LampBlockers indexes captured structural geometry in a stackless bounding-volume tree. StructuralRectangles merges suitable coplanar triangles into rectangles while retaining geometric holes. Intersection uses precomputed plane and coordinate rows. Geometry is cached until the relevant uploaded scene geometry changes; visibility is still queried for contributing light/sample pairs.

The capture rule selects wall objects and game-object placement types 9 and 12–21. It does not capture general furniture, actors, terrain or floor surfaces as point blockers. Roof cutaways remain blockers even when hidden for viewing. That is the implemented approximation to “walls and roofs only”; it is not an audited semantic classification of every game asset.

Important limits and interactions:

  • A fence represented by a selected wall object can be a point blocker, even though the room detector excludes fences. The two wall definitions differ.
  • The capture uses opaque faces. Transparent structural faces and models omitted before capture need inspection in real scenes.
  • The ray test is double-sided, has one world unit of endpoint slack and offsets the surface by two units. These tolerances trade self-blocking against small leaks near seams.
  • Wall-mounted lamp origins still use legacy wall-placement helpers. The visibility origin can differ from the authored visible light center.
  • Door/model changes depend on scene geometry invalidation and upload. The code path alone does not prove every animated door updates its blocker at the right moment.
  • Props and actors can still darken point-lit surfaces through AO. That is not their geometry entering the point-light ray test.
  • Shared lamp queries serve opaque surfaces, transparent surfaces, water, fog and weather, but those consumers shade/integrate the returned light differently.
Lighting & indoorsSun, moon and directional shadows

DayCycle advances the visible sun altitude along a 70*sin(phase)+12 degree curve. The moon is opposite the sun's azimuth with negated altitude. The sun is the directional caster above −3° sun altitude; below that, the moon is the caster. Lightning can temporarily replace the caster direction and light.

The shadow direction deliberately differs from the visible celestial disc: its elevation is clamped to at least 30°, at most 68° for the sun and 58° for the moon. This avoids very long low-angle shadows but means the visible moon's location is not a literal prediction of shadow geometry.

FrameState enables directional shadows only when the scene is outdoor-classified, Sun and moon lighting is enabled, Shadows is enabled, and the client is logged in. ShadowPass additionally needs initialized resources and scene state. Both sides of geometry cast; general props/actors can cast these directional shadows. The wall/roof-only requirement currently concerns point lights.

The default 32-tile shadow distance defines the covered area. At a fixed map resolution, increasing it spreads texels over a larger area and changes the rendered geometry set. Resolution choices are 1024, 2048 and 4096. Static caching and dynamic rendering reduce some repeated work, but this is not a universal fixed-cost pass. Leaf shadow sway is currently hardcoded off even when visible foliage moves. The emissive-strength setting also changes whether emissive surfaces cast in this pass.

The code contains a moon-shadow path. That does not establish that the user's reported absent nighttime shadows are fixed or visually acceptable. A useful live check must confirm scene classification, caster selection, shadow-map validity, receiver lighting and the actual resulting shadow. Warm fill, sky ambient, AO and overlapping lamps can hide directional contrast even when a map exists. These are hypotheses to distinguish, not a diagnosis from this audit.

sky.glsl uses nominal sun intensity 2.9 and moon intensity 0.675, with different color and altitude fades. Moon color is blue (0.42, 0.55, 1). There are reversed-edge smoothstep calls in celestial fading; they deserve a deliberate PC/Mac portability review rather than an assumption that the visual result is identical on both drivers.

Lighting & indoorsSkylight and AO

VisibilityFrame prepares skylight for the top-level world view when outdoor lighting is active and the client is logged in. It is distinct from point-light blockers. Its voxel model may include props/vegetation that should not block point lights.

skylight_ao.glsl combines directional sky visibility with wall-aware interpolation and an opening field. The field reaches three tiles from openings. The sampled grid has 12 height layers, a first sampled height of 40 units and 72-unit spacing. Directional components are weighted by the surface normal; diagonal wall halves and walls limit interpolation across partitions. Previous/current data is blended during updates. skylight_ambient.glsl keeps a 0.40 floor and defaults to open visibility when skylight is unavailable.

ao_frag.glsl computes AO from screen depth at half resolution with 6, 8 or 12 samples. It cannot see hidden/off-screen geometry, so this is a view-dependent proximity approximation. Default radius 75% of a tile equals 96 world units. Its thickness and bias scale with radius, so radius changes the character of occlusion as well as its reach.

AO strength applies clamp(1 - (1 - ao) * strength, 0, 1). The current direct-light AO coefficient is 1: AO fully multiplies direct sun/moon diffuse, point diffuse, sky ambient and room fill. Leaf transmission is treated separately. Setting AO off does not necessarily remove the whole half-resolution pass, because directional shadow and skylight values also travel through it.

Lighting & indoorsTransparent surfaces do not receive identical lighting

frag.glsl shades the alpha pass with its own outdoor ambient/sky/direct formula and shared lamp visibility. That formula does not add the opaque pass's room fill, voxel skylight or SSAO. It samples the previous frame's directional shadow map/matrix. Cave lighting is also a separate branch there.

Consequently an indoor transparent object can have a different apparent brightness from an adjacent opaque object even with identical imported lamps. Water has further specialized lighting. These are actual pipeline differences, not proof of a point-occlusion defect.

Lighting & indoorsReloads, remaining complexity and performance uncertainty

SceneLights clears state on loading/login transitions and subsequently recollects definitions against scene entities. Lamp lifetime/fade rules, building workers, room-texture packing, skylight rebuilds and structural geometry upload are independent update paths. Replacing point visibility does not automatically eliminate visible light repopulation or all asynchronous transitions.

Active point visibility now uses LampBlockers. LampVolume is test-only; LampFrame.lightMapOff is a deprecated replay option and runtime light-map baking is no longer called. LightMap, LampCubes, old cluster upload hooks and some bake metadata still exist as production leftovers. They should not be documented as active point-shadow techniques merely because classes remain.

One concrete leftover worth reviewing: VisibilityFrame.prepareVoxels still requests voxel preparation when Point lights is enabled, even though the new point blocker query no longer uses that general voxel representation. This is a code dependency observation, not a measured explanation for a millisecond result.

The new method's likely work drivers are visible sample count, overlapping lights per sample, how soon a ray is rejected, traversed tree nodes/primitives, structural upload/rebuild frequency, and which passes request lamp light. There is no per-light visibility bake amortizing all of those queries. A synthetic full-screen overlapping-light timing cannot by itself establish typical game cost, the cause of a slowdown, or acceptable playability. No new timing was performed for this document.

Sky & weatherHow the parts connect

FrameState selects environment/time, then WeatherFrame updates the climate and spatial weather field. That field drives clouds, precipitation, surface wetness/snow, atmospheric fog, wind and rain audio. SkyFrame updates the visible sky, night sky and cached sky lighting. The composite combines surface weather with the normal lighting pipeline; fog is another pass. These are overlapping systems, not a single weather shader.

Sources: FrameState.java, WeatherFrame.java, SkyFrame.java.

The weather focus is normally the player's position; camera coordinates are a fallback. Area environment selection uses the camera focal point. This distinction matters with a detached camera or at an area boundary: “the biome I see” and “the rain/audio at my character” need not be identical.

Sand and ash reuse flakes but override size, speed, drift and tint, and suppress snow/wet accumulation. Editing one JSON field can therefore change multiple effects.

Sky & weatherLocal weather and region boundaries

The active field has 33×33 cells, each 16 tiles wide. Cell state is keyed to world location, so recentering preserves overlapping cells. Scene/base/plane/area-mode changes can rebuild layout. Instance locations are mapped to known world coordinates when available, otherwise use the focus biome.

Weather channels are spatially blurred (Gaussian radius 5 cells, sigma 2), ground uses a radius-2 weighted kernel, and sky weather a wider radius-6/sigma-3 kernel. Consequently the system deliberately produces broad boundary mixtures rather than weather changing at a sharp region line. Disabling buildup bypasses temporal easing, not these spatial kernels.

First-use cells can snap to their current target. Forced rain/snow without buildup can immediately initialize fully wet/white ground. This behavior differs from automatic mode's reconstructed ground history. Weather texture channels are stored in 8-bit fields; the ground exposure result used by the composite is a separate half-resolution floating-point buffer.

Sources: WeatherField.java, Weather.java.

Sky & weatherTemperature, snow and wetness

Automatic mode simulates a regional history in hourly weather-clock steps. It reconstructs two seasons of history, bounded to 4–30 game days, rather than storing actual footprints of every past shower. Temperature combines seasonal means, a daily swing (coldest near 03:00, warmest near 15:00), cloud damping, precipitation cooling and slow variation. Frozen regions enforce a cold ceiling and at least 30 cm snow.

Precipitation type is temperature-dependent: the snow-share curve runs from all snow at −0.5°C to all rain at 2.5°C, but withTemperature selects the whole rain or snow weather type at share 0.5. It is not a continuously mixed sleet renderer. Biomes marked never-snow stay rainy. A freezing storm can become the Snow catalog entry, changing more than just the particle texture.

Ground rules include 5 cm snow per game hour per unit snowfall, a 60 cm cap, and full visual cover at 10 cm. Above freezing, snow melts with temperature, rain and sunlight; excess deep snow also settles. Wetness builds from rain and dries faster with sun/warmer air. Snowmelt intentionally does not add wetness. Manual-mode fallback accumulation uses separate timescales: approximately 25 s wet buildup, 240 s snow buildup, 120 s drying and 900 s melting, rather than the automatic historical climate model.

The automatic historical simulation uses catalog precipitation and shower share, not the UI intensity multiplier. The visible local field uses UI-scaled precipitation and gates growth toward historical cover when rain/snow is locally present. Therefore intensity is not a single physically consistent control over both visible snowfall and long-term snow depth.

Sources: Climate.java, WeatherClimate.java, WeatherField.java.

Sky & weatherParticles, wind and sound

Rain/snow positions are procedural and world-anchored, rather than individually persistent simulated particles. The renderer uses distance layers: nearest-layer budgets are 24,000 rain instances and 12,000 snow instances at full amount, with up to six layers and separate impact rendering (maximum 4,400). These are configured drawing budgets, not measured simultaneous visible counts or GPU costs. Layers change size/coverage with distance, and transitions hide boundaries.

Wind displacement is integrated to keep gust changes from teleporting particles. Rain/snow falling and drift coordinates wrap on a 600-second interval. Cloud drift also uses the shared Wind driver. Particle drift has a base plus wind/intensity scaling; catalog sand/ash change flake motion independently. Turning off scene sway does not mean the whole world has zero wind.

Rain audio samples local rain and rings at 1,200/2,800/4,800 world units in eight directions. Rain can therefore be audible while approaching a wet region, before particles reach the player. Loudness is multiplied by weather volume and game sound-effect volume, then sheltered/muffled. Thunder events are scheduled separately; the lightning/rumble visual state can proceed even when the user's volume is zero.

Sources: Weather.java, Wind.java, Clouds.java, WeatherFrame.java, WeatherAudio.java.

Sky & weatherFog and haze are several effects

The volume pass works at half resolution, then filters/upsamples. Its base extinction is configDensity * 1e-5; the frame multiplies that by weather fog and an area-fog factor derived from 117 HD's fog depth (the area-depth ratio is capped at 2). Fog height is normally 3 tiles times the weather height multiplier, minimum 16 units. It falls off vertically, fades near the camera over the first 7 tiles and at its outer edge over 7 tiles; coverage radius is at most 40 tiles or the configured draw distance. Ground-height data is prepared by a worker and blended when changed.

Morning/evening mist is another weather calculation, not simply the Fog catalog type. Humidity drives dawn mist, with smaller dusk/night contributions; dry climates can gain midday haze. Dawn builds around 03:00–06:00 and clears by about 10:30 weather-clock time. Weather turbulence/cloud state modifies the allowance. The fog multiplier uses a saturation knee/cap to prevent unbounded addition.

Distance fog, area environment fog, sky haze/desaturation and the volumetric pass have distinct controls and consumers. Disabling volumetric fog does not imply a perfectly clear horizon; choosing Clear does not eliminate humid dawn mist. Cave/closed-sky behavior also has its own weather fog fallback.

Sources: FogClimate.java, VolumetricFog.java, FrameState.java, SkyFrame.java.

Sky & weatherDay, moon, cloud lighting and lightning

The sun follows a stylized altitude curve (70*sin(phase*2π) + 12 degrees), not a geographic astronomy model. The moon is opposite. The directional caster changes from sun to moon when solar altitude falls below −3°. Shadow-casting elevation is clamped to 30–68° for sun and 30–58° for moon to prevent extremely long horizon shadows. The visible celestial body and effective shadow direction therefore intentionally differ near the horizon.

Direct strength fades across twilight and is reduced by overcast. At full overcast the multiplier retains 30% for rain-like cover or 42% for snowy cover. Moon strength is weaker than daytime strength. This confirms a code path for moon directional lighting; it does not prove that shadows are visibly correct in the live scene. See the lighting feature map for shadow-map scheduling, occlusion and indoor lighting.

Lightning is a temporary directional caster, not a point light placed in the world. Storm strength must exceed 0.6. Scheduling uses five-second real-time windows with 0.5 chance; a strike can wait up to a second for its shadow map before being dropped. Main flash rise/hold/fall are 0.05/0.4/0.4 seconds, with secondary flare and thunder/rumble modulation. Its direction is elevated 30–40°, at a nominal distance of 3,000–9,000 units. These conditions explain why enabling lightning or choosing a storm need not yield an immediate visible flash.

Sources: DayCycle.java, Lightning.java.

Sky & weatherNight sky and cached sky lighting

Stars rotate once per visible day around a pole tilted 35° from vertical. Meteors are scheduled in 12-second real-time slots with 45% chance, lasting 0.7–1.2 seconds and beginning near the camera's view. Aurora is normally limited to TUNDRA/ARCTIC; 30% of nights have none and other nights have varying strength. Strength transitions over about six seconds and is multiplied by a fixed 0.396 visibility factor. Its night schedule uses visible game time, not the separate fixed-speed weather clock. Developer auroraHold and DayCycle.heldHour can override normal behavior and should be checked before judging settings.

Sky lighting is cached separately from drawing the sky. SkyFrame includes quantized weather/settings/time in cache inputs and retains old cached sky across loading, easing back toward analytic lighting if captures stop. Consequently a visual sky change, a cache update and the light falling on objects are related but not necessarily simultaneous operations.

Sources: NightSky.java, SkyFrame.java.

Sky & weatherConfirmed parameter issues versus things still to verify

Confirmed from code:

  • Two config descriptions are attached to the wrong concepts (skyHorizonAtEdge, weatherSounds). The volume-fog implementation comment also mentions a 25% default, while current configuration declares 15 with maximum 15.
  • The intensity tooltip emphasizes automatic weather, but the same /0.6 scaling applies to forced precipitation. Ground history is not scaled by that slider in automatic mode.
  • Fixed visual time does not freeze climate or morning mist. This may be intentional, but it must be visible in the product's explanation.
  • “Off” and “Clear” resolve to clear weather rather than disabling the whole atmosphere stack. “Buildup off” removes temporal smoothing, not boundary blending.
  • Indoor shelter, audio muffling, fog density and point-light blockers use different geometry/data and thresholds. One subsystem's success cannot verify the others.
  • Snow/rain conversion swaps catalog types rather than continuously mixing the precipitation; sand/ash share the snow rendering channel but do not accumulate snow.

Not established by this audit: whether any transition actually flickers, whether fog is visually excessive on the user's saved settings, whether the 600-second coordinate wrap is visible, whether hidden-roof exposure is correct for every model, or which effect dominates measured frame time. Those need live scene comparison and scoped timing; none follows merely from the number of code paths or configured particle counts.

Useful player review cases are: one doorway with visible/hidden roof; forced rain then clear to inspect residual puddles; forced snow then thaw; fixed noon observed across weather-clock dawn; a dry region against a humid one; the same night with clear/overcast and sun/moon shadows enabled. These are proposed acceptance checks, not tests already performed.

Rendering & waterScene loading, distance and map hiding

drawDistance is 50 tiles by default, adjustable 0–184. It controls visibility distance, while expanded map loading adds actual map chunks available to render: default three extra 8-tile chunks, range zero to five. Their costs are related but they are not interchangeable. The edge fade is centered on camera focal position and the requested client scene distance accounts for camera offset.

Scene loading builds an atomic derived bundle of scene zones, roof changes, water and fog-ground data. Voxel and static-shadow builds may finish later; old derived state can stand in until they complete. Reusing zones avoids rebuilding everything, but introduces dependencies between cached geometry and derived lighting/water state. This is an important place to inspect reload symptoms before treating them as shader problems.

CPU upload preparation has a separate fixed worker count of up to three, derived from CPU availability. This is not the numThreads setting: that setting defaults to three (0–15) and rebuilds the dynamic-model rendering worker structures plus GPU flags.

OpenGL scene jobs run on the client thread after presentation, prioritized LOAD, SHOWN, UPKEEP. Normal deadline is 1 ms; load work during the loading state gets 4 ms. The deadline is cooperative and each step can execute at least one unit, so these are scheduling budgets, not proven upper bounds on frame cost. Exceptions log and end that job rather than guaranteeing successful completion.

Rendering & waterConcrete simplification and documentation issues
  • Correct misleading tooltips: skyHorizonAtEdge currently describes automatic/fixed weather; weatherSounds describes water surface/bed. The point-light tooltip's blanket statement about no shadows also needs reconciling with wall/roof occlusion terminology.
  • Correct WaterMap's obsolete no-surface-geometry explanation. Following that comment leads a reader to the wrong rendering model.
  • Make setting change semantics explicit: live uniform, shader rebuild, target resize, scene reload or next natural reload. hideUnrelatedMaps is a concrete inconsistency to decide on.
  • Separate measurement modes in reporting: paced frame intervals, normal GPU queries and deliberately serialized GPU queries answer different questions. Never present them under an undifferentiated 'frame time'.
  • Keep surface emissiveness, placed point lights, room fill and thin-leaf transmission separate in both naming and controls. Similar visible brightness does not mean they share a light source.
  • Centralize water appearance constants only after identifying which artistic controls should exist. A wholesale abstraction without a clear behavior model would add complexity.
  • Do not infer dead code solely from class names or stale comments. This audit confirms contradictory documentation, but does not establish that entire legacy subsystems can safely be deleted. Trace callers and resource ownership before removal.

Visual acceptance remains open: inspect the user's ordinary playable scene, water at banks and waterfalls, thin transparent edges, swaying vegetation, map boundaries and changes between indoor/outdoor areas. Source inspection explains expected behavior; it does not certify the result looks good.
